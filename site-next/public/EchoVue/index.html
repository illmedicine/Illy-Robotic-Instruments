How it works

Three steps. No camera in any of them.

01

Place a bridge

A small WiFi sensor goes in a hallway, lobby, pool or laundry. It joins the WiFi you already have. There is nothing to calibrate and nobody to walk around the room.

02

It senses motion

The bridge reads how a body disturbs the radio signal and boils it down to a few numbers a second, on the device. Raw signal never leaves it, and no image ever exists.

03

See it live

The dashboard shows which areas are occupied right now, how many people, devices and pets passed through over time, and what the pattern suggests.

Privacy by design

A policy can be broken. A system that cannot do something is harder to break.

No camera, no images

There is no camera input in the product at all. Occupancy was the goal, not identity.

Public areas only

Hallways, lobby, pool and laundry. The backend rejects any sensing zone named like a room, suite or bathroom.

Features, not raw signal

Each bridge reduces the radio signal to a few motion features per second before anything is sent.

Anonymised device counts

Bluetooth addresses are hashed with a salt that changes daily. The dashboard sees counts, never addresses.

Estimates say so

Every figure carries a likely range and a confidence level. With no usable signal, the screen says so instead of guessing.

Short, aggregated memory

History is anonymous per-minute counts, kept 90 days. No raw radio data, no addresses, no identities.

History & insights

Not just who is there now. What the pattern means.

  • Estimated people, devices and pets that passed through an area, over an hour, a day or since the bridge last restarted.
  • Phones that rotate their Bluetooth address are recognised as one device, so counts are not inflated.
  • Busiest hours, how long people linger, and which areas deserve attention.
  • Plain-language observations written by AI from aggregated counts only. No device ids or personal data are sent.
